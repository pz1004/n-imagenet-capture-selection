# Product fusion and stacking on the transfer trajectories

This retrospective comparison uses the archived released Event Histogram logits of the vertical (variant 1) and horizontal (variant 2) transfer paths in the hash-placed, up-to-30,000-event window. The existing split contains ten calibration and forty evaluation photographs per class for 900 classes. All 1,000 predictions remain eligible. The first scan is vertical. Older aggregate results and evaluation source identities are known.

For product fusion, fit one inverse temperature per path on 9,000 calibration images by mean multiclass negative log likelihood. Predict the class that maximizes the sum of the two temperature-adjusted logits. For stacking, each class has three features, the two natural logarithms of its unscaled softmax probabilities and their minimum. Fit three class-shared unrestricted weights by minimizing the mean multiclass negative log likelihood plus 0.005 times their sum of squares, giving a ridge penalty of 0.01 in the gradient. No intercept is needed since a class-shared intercept cancels. The penalty, features and models are fixed before computing these new evaluation decisions.

Comparators are first only, the archived procedure for a local discordance-trained logistic selector (with a free intercept and the historical slope penalty), and averaging the raw probability vectors. Compare all methods at full rescan coverage and at a common exact budget of 10,800 lowest-confidence vertical scores among 36,000 cohort images. Retain the first class on unrequested images. Score top-1 after using the smallest class index in a tie. For each policy and budget report absolute accuracy, rescues and losses against first alone, newly correct predictions when both individual top classes are wrong, and incorrect decisions when either scan was correct. Distinguish the latter on requested images from failures over the full cohort.

Report paired percentage-point differences between the two fusion alternatives and each comparator, and against each other. Estimate 95% percentile intervals from 2,000 separate within-class calibration/test source bootstrap draws and 2,000 whole-class draws shared between the two splits. All draws share images across arms. Refit both temperatures, the stack and the local selector, and rerank the request quota, in every draw. Freeze the numerical procedure with an exact likelihood-gradient convergence check. Compare reported results with independently reconstructed full-class scores and existing baseline decisions. No calibration labels from the test split enter a fit.
